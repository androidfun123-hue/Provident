Why People Use SRS

A voluntary retirement account that sits alongside CPF.

Lower Tax Now

Each dollar you put in comes off your taxable income, up to the yearly cap.

Tax-Free Growth

Investment gains inside SRS aren't taxed while they stay in the account.

Half-Taxed in Retirement

From your SRS retirement age, only 50% of each withdrawal counts as income, over up to 10 years.

Common Questions

Is SRS worth it for me?

It's most useful when your top tax rate is 7% or higher (chargeable income above S$40,000). Below that, the yearly saving is small and the money is locked up until retirement unless you pay a penalty. The calculator shows your top rate.

What happens if I just leave the money as cash?

It earns very little interest. Most people invest SRS money in things like Singapore Savings Bonds, unit trusts, ETFs, shares or insurance plans to make it grow.

Can SRS be used for insurance?

Yes. Some single-premium endowment and annuity plans can be bought with SRS money. Ask us which plans fit your retirement timeline.

Does SRS reduce my CPF contributions?

No. SRS is separate from CPF and voluntary. It doesn't change what you or your employer put into CPF.

Why is the S$80,000 relief cap important?

All your personal reliefs combined, including SRS, can't exceed S$80,000 a year. If you already claim close to that, an SRS top-up may save little or nothing. The calculator applies this cap.
