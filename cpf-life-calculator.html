CPF LIFE Calculator

Made simple — for every Singaporean, whether you're a freelancer just starting to build up CPF, an employee with steady contributions, or getting close to 55. Type in your age and how much you have in CPF now. This tool shows you where you stand today, and what your CPF could grow into by the time you retire.

You don't need a lot of CPF savings to use this tool. Most people aim for the Basic Retirement Sum first — it's the smallest of CPF's three savings goals, and it's still enough to get a monthly payout for life. This calculator is built around that goal, not the biggest one.
This is a simple estimate, not an official CPF number. We built this tool to help you plan. It is not made by, or connected to, the CPF Board. It uses simple, rounded numbers instead of CPF's exact rules, so your real CPF LIFE payout may be different — it depends on interest rates, your gender, and your exact balance on the day payouts start. For your real numbers, use the official CPF LIFE Estimator. This is not financial advice.

3 CPF savings goals

CPF has 3 target amounts. You only need to reach the first one to join CPF LIFE.
Basic Retirement Sum (BRS)$110,200
Full Retirement Sum (FRS)$220,400
Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS)$440,800
Reaching the Basic Retirement Sum (BRS) is a good first goal for most people — it already gets you a monthly payout for life. The Full Retirement Sum (FRS) and Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS) goals just mean a bigger payout on top of that. Turn on “Aim for a bigger goal?” on the left if you want to see what a higher target looks like.

How you get paid

Once your monthly payouts start, you can choose how they're paid out.
Basic PlanSmaller amount, biggest bequest
Standard Plan (Default)Same amount every month, moderate bequest
Escalating PlanStarts smaller, grows ~2%/yr
This is separate from the 3 savings goals above — it only changes how your payout is paid out, not how much you've saved. Note: CPF uses the word “Basic” for both the Basic Retirement Sum (a savings goal) and the Basic Plan (a payout plan). They are not the same thing.
What's a bequest? If you pass away before your CPF LIFE savings (plus interest, minus payouts already received) are fully paid out, CPF pays what's left to your nominated beneficiaries — this is called a bequest, or death benefit. All 3 plans include one; the Basic Plan is built to leave the biggest, since it puts less of your savings into your monthly payout. You nominate who receives it through CPF's Nomination Scheme.

Advanced: how fast CPF grows

You can skip this section — the numbers below are already reasonable defaults.
CPF goals grow by 3.5%/yr
The 3 savings goals (BRS/FRS/ERS) go up a little most years. 3.5% a year is a reasonable guess.
Interest is fixed at what CPF guarantees: 2.5%/yr on OA, 4%/yr on SA and your Retirement Account. On top of that, CPF gives extra interest on your first $60,000 of combined savings (more after 55) — this tool includes that bonus too, since it helps the most if you have less CPF.

How we worked this out

Under 55, this model transfers SA first, then OA, up to that year's projected Full Retirement Sum (FRS) to form your Retirement Account (RA); any leftover stays in OA. At 55 or older, CPF has already done that transfer for you, so the RA balance you enter is used directly instead. If “Aim for a bigger goal?” is on, leftover OA is used first (at 55, or immediately if you're already 55+), then the model rechecks the gap each later year against that year's rising Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS) goal. On top of the guaranteed 2.5%/4% floor rates, CPF also pays extra interest on smaller balances — an extra 1%/yr on the first $60,000 of combined OA and SA (or RA) savings, and a further 1%/yr on the first $30,000 once you're 55 or older — and this model includes that bonus. Your MediSave (MA) balance is for reference only here — under CPF's own rules it's last in line for that bonus interest (after OA and SA/RA), so it doesn't change any figure on this page. Contribution growth uses the single annual OA/SA amounts you set, held flat in today's dollars — it does not model CPF's age-banded contribution or allocation-rate tables. The monthly payout is estimated by scaling your projected RA-at-65 against CPF Board's own published reference points — the RA balances members following the Basic, Full and Enhanced Retirement Sum path are projected to hold at 65, and the matching 2026-cohort Standard Plan payout examples at each ($950 / $1,780 / $3,440, among other published points) — interpolated in between. Deferring past 65 assumes a 7%-per-year compounding increase, consistent with CPF's published deferral bonus; ages 63–64 are a mirrored extrapolation, since CPF LIFE payouts officially start from 65. Basic and Escalating plan figures apply a rough illustrative adjustment (Escalating starts ~20% lower and grows 2%/yr; Basic starts ~10% lower) since CPF Board does not publish an exact plan-to-plan formula — use the official CPF LIFE Estimator to compare real payout amounts for your plan choice. Reference anchors: CPF Board's published 2026 Retirement Sums and Standard Plan payout examples.
